## Goal
camt.053 and camt.052 entries that the bank marks as reversals (`RvslInd` true) are imported as money coming back, a refund, and not as another new expense. A reversed payment of 25.00 that arrives as money in (CRDT) is preselected as a Refund in the Categorise step (owner decision 23), and the import warns how many reversal entries the file holds so the person can check them. Today the flag is never read: the reversal is just another row (original 25.00 out plus a reversal 25.00 gives money out 50.00 in one reading, or money in suggested as Income in the other).

## Notes
Source: D-bank.md problem 10 (reversals; VERIFIED behaviour, real-bank semantics UNVERIFIED; probes p07_camt.mjs C2, p11_camt2.mjs C2b), independently confirmed in D-bank-verify.md item 7 (probe c7.mjs; `grep -ri "RvslInd\|reversal" src/` returns nothing). Owner decision 23 (research/3.0/decisions-3.0.md) replaces the earlier "skip and warn" design with "import as a refund".

UNVERIFIED, the one assumption of this card: how the bank sets `CdtDbtInd` on a reversal entry. Banks and national guidelines differ and it could not be checked offline. This card reads it as the direction of the entry's own money movement (the plain reading): a reversed payment arrives as `CRDT` with `RvslInd` true, money in. If the owner's sample shows the other convention (the reversal of a payment keeps `DBIT`), the whole change is one line in `camtEntry`: flip the direction of rows read with `RvslInd` true. Do not build both now. Verify with the owner's sample file before the card is called done.

Cause: `camtEntry` (src/import/xml.js:472-527) reads status, `CdtDbtInd`, date and amount but never `RvslInd`; `statementRow` (src/import/types.js) has no way to carry the flag; `defaultDecisions` (src/import/core.js:354) picks kind 'income' for every money-in row unless a rule says otherwise.

Fix:
- `camtEntry`: after the status check (:473-476) read `RvslInd` (text, case-insensitive; 'true' or '1' means reversal). Direction, date, amount and the other fields are read exactly as today. Pass `reversal: true` to `statementRow` and add 1 to a new `counts.reversal` (add `reversal: 0` to the counts object in `parseCamt`, :543).
- `statementRow` (types.js): carry `reversal: true` in the returned object ONLY when `fields.reversal === true`; ordinary rows keep exactly today's keys (importXml.test.js deepEquals rows). Add the optional property to the StatementRow typedef.
- `skippedWarnings` (xml.js:354-369): add `${plural(n, 'entry is a reversal', 'entries are reversals')} marked by the bank. Check ${n === 1 ? 'it' : 'them'}.` (neutral on purpose: a reversal of an incoming payment is money out). The review step already shows the warning array (acceptLoaded passes `result.warnings`). Keep the existing warning texts unchanged and put the new one after them.
- `defaultDecisions` (core.js:354-369): for a row with `reversal === true` and `direction === 'in'` the kind is 'refund' (instead of 'income'). The category is the rule's category when a rule or the shop-rule lookup in `suggestedRule` (:343-348) finds one, else Uncategorised. A reversal row with direction 'out' (a reversed incoming payment) keeps today's default, kind 'expense', so it is visible and the person can change it to Skip or Transfer.
- The row text, fingerprint and duplicate matching are unchanged: the reversal row has its own direction, so it does not match the original payment; a re-import of the same statement matches it as Exact (same fingerprint, stored as a refund entry, `storedEntries` in core.js treats `refund === true` as money in).
- Batch entries: C-067 splits an entry into several rows and must carry the `reversal` flag into each part.
