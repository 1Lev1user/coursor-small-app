## Goal
Two encodings are read wrongly today (research/3.0/D-bank.md section 14, VERIFIED):
- UTF-16 text without a byte-order mark is decoded as UTF-8 full of NUL characters, so 0 rows are read;
- a windows-1251 file (Russian-language bank export) is decoded as windows-1257 and every Cyrillic letter becomes garbage, with no warning.
After this card both are detected. UTF-8, UTF-8 with BOM, UTF-16 with BOM and windows-1257 keep working as today.

## Notes
- UTF-16 without BOM: if at least 30% of the odd bytes (LE) or even bytes (BE) in the first 4 KB are 0x00, decode as UTF-16LE or BE.
- windows-1251 vs windows-1257: both are single-byte. When strict UTF-8 fails, decode with both. Pick 1251 when its text has more Cyrillic letters (U+0400-U+04FF) in the first 4 KB than 1257's text has Baltic letters (āčēģīķļņšūž and capitals), and at least 20 Cyrillic letters. Otherwise keep 1257 (today's default).
- The returned `encoding` gains 'windows-1251'. Check where `encoding` is shown or saved (layouts in import.js) and that an unknown value cannot break it.
- Tests:
  - UTF-16LE and UTF-16BE without BOM;
  - a Russian windows-1251 CSV ("Дата;Описание;Сумма");
  - a Latvian windows-1257 CSV stays 1257;
  - pure ASCII stays utf-8;
  - all bytes are built in the test with made-up text.

Amendment 2026-10-18 (review of PR #104): the rule "more Cyrillic than Baltic letters" flips a Latvian windows-1257 file with one foreign letter (Café, Strauß) to windows-1251. New rule: pick 1251 only when the 1251 reading has at least 20 Cyrillic letters AND those are at least half of all letters in it (Cyrillic plus A-Z/a-z) AND at least twice the Baltic count of the 1257 reading. Russian text is mostly Cyrillic; Latvian, Lithuanian, Estonian and Western texts are mostly A-Z.
